15 Best SaaS Website Design Agencies: Reviewed and Compared

Draft for review. Not published. 14 Oct 2026.

If you are shortlisting a SaaS website design agency, you are probably looking at three things: who has built sites for companies like yours, what it will cost, and how the agency runs the work.

The benchmark figures on this page come from a SaaS client who was choosing an agency to redesign their website. The client sent the same brief to a long list of website design agencies: a five-page website with copywriting and a Webflow or Framer build. Each agency then walked through its price, scope and process on a sales call, and every call was sat through end to end, so you don't have to. The full data is in the State of UI/UX Design Agencies 2026 report.

The agencies' pricing ran from $1,500 to $120,000, with a median of $25,000. The gap in price came from what each quote included: copywriting, brand work, the build and how senior the team was.

The 15 SaaS website design agencies below are reviewed and compared on the same criteria: shipped SaaS websites, platform, starting price, hourly rate, team size, verified Clutch reviews and founders' LinkedIn posts.

How each agency was reviewed

Every agency on this list was assessed against the same evidence a buyer can check, with the rigour of The State of UI/UX Design Agencies 2026, where a SaaS client put the same brief to 57 agencies. Each had to clear three bars:

  • A strong, verified track record. Consistent, recent client reviews on Clutch, or a portfolio of shipped websites.
  • Real SaaS work. Named software clients on the agency's site, in case studies or in verified reviews. Each agency has shipped website work for software companies.
  • Published facts. Minimum project size, hourly rate, team size and location from Clutch, checked on October 14, 2026. Stack, copywriting and motion come from each agency's own site. Outcome numbers appear only where a client stated them in a Clutch review or the agency published them in a case study, and each one says which.

Agencies are ordered by fit for funded B2B SaaS companies hiring for the website. Pricing and timeline benchmarks come from the same report, where a SaaS client put the same brief to 57 agencies; not every agency gave a price or timeline.

What moves the price

  • The homepage visual system. Most of the design cost sits in finding the visual direction and the homepage; later pages reuse its sections, so cutting pages saves less than expected.
  • Copy. Whether the agency writes the site, including positioning.
  • Product visuals and motion. Every animated state of a product creative has to be designed before it is animated, so custom UI loops and 3D add the most cost per page.
  • Migration. Moving an existing blog and keeping its rankings needs a redirect map and QA, not just design.
  • Integrations. HubSpot, Salesforce, analytics, consent management and localisation each add build time.

How to tell two similar agencies apart

A good SaaS website agency owns the whole path from "what should the site say" to "the site is live and marketing can edit it": positioning, discovery, copy, design, build and launch. Two agencies can both claim that and still differ on who does the work and what happens after launch.

Write the brief before talking to agencies

Every agency in the benchmark received the same website brief: 5 pages, copywriting, and development on Webflow or Framer. The spread from $1,500 to $120,000 came from how each agency read it. A one-page brief makes quotes comparable:

  • The pages needed at launch, and the ones that can wait.
  • Who writes the copy, and whether positioning is settled.
  • The preferred stack, or the reason it is open.
  • Integrations: CRM, analytics, consent, localisation.
  • URLs that must keep ranking.
  • The one decision-maker and the feedback turnaround they can commit to.
  • The launch date, and what happens if it moves.

Match the agency to the trigger

  • The site no longer reflects the company. Look for positioning, brand and website in one team. A pure build shop will reproduce the old message in a new layout.
  • A launch or a raise has a date. Look for a small senior team with a published timeline and a start date within two weeks.
  • The designer left. Look for a retainer or subscription that covers ongoing pages, not another fixed project.
  • Enterprise buyers are now reviewing the company. Look for experience with security, integrations and use-case pages, not just a striking homepage.
  • The trigger sits in the product, not the website. This list covers websites. If the app itself needs product design, the lists of UX design agencies and product design agencies are the closer match. When the site and the app have drifted apart, a shared design system is what brings them back to one look.

Twelve questions to ask before signing

  • Who actually does the work, and are they employees or contractors?
  • Who writes the copy, and is positioning included?
  • Which three shipped SaaS sites are closest to this one, and what changed for those companies after launch?
  • Which platform fits this company, and why not the alternatives?
  • How many rounds of revisions are included, and what counts as a round?
  • What does the agency need from the client team each week?
  • How are redirects and SEO handled during the move?
  • Who owns the Webflow or Framer workspace and the Figma files at the end?
  • What does support cost after launch?
  • Can the agency share two past clients to call?
  • What does a handoff file contain, and who checks the build against the design?

Red flags

  • A portfolio of Dribbble concepts and no live SaaS sites with pricing, integrations or docs.
  • "Conversion-focused" without being able to say which numbers changed for a client.
  • A proposal that starts with visual design and skips positioning and page structure.
  • No answer to who edits the site after launch, or who owns the workspace.
  • A fixed price with no clear definition of a revision round.
  • Dozens of unreasoned design options in place of a few directions with a rationale.
  • Senior designers in the pitch and junior designers on the project. Ask who will be assigned, and whether they work only on your account.

Most teams want both conversion results and a site that looks better than their competitors'. Choose an agency whose portfolio shows taste and whose case studies show numbers; be cautious of one that can show only one.

FAQs

How much does it cost to hire a SaaS website design agency?

For a 5-page site with copy and a Webflow or Framer build, a SaaS client put the same brief to 57 agencies, and the median quote was $25,000. Not every agency gave a price; of the agencies that quoted, 58% quoted between $15,000 and $50,000, 25% under $10,000 and 17% above $50,000. Larger sites, migrations, custom illustration and motion move the price up.

How long does a SaaS website redesign take?

Most agencies quote 5 to 9 weeks for a 5-page site (43.5% of the benchmark). Add the agency's lead time, which is two to four weeks for 45% of agencies. Bricx takes websites live in about five weeks.

What is the best website builder for a SaaS company?

Webflow, for most B2B SaaS marketing sites: 65% of benchmarked agencies build on it (agencies could name more than one platform), and marketing can publish without engineers. Framer suits smaller, design-led sites that change often. WordPress suits teams with a large content library and in-house developers. Headless builds suit sites that share components or data with the product.

Should a SaaS company hire a specialist or a general web design agency?

A specialist already knows how pricing, integrations, security and demo pages work, and what a buying committee looks for. A general agency can produce an attractive site that misses those pages. For a funded B2B SaaS company, a specialist such as those listed above is usually the faster path.

Who maintains the website after launch?

Agree this before signing. Among benchmarked agencies, 40% offer a monthly retainer for post-launch support, 35% bill hourly and 25% sell a prepaid bank of hours. A good handover also includes documentation and a component library so an in-house team can take over.

Who owns the website and design files?

The client should. Confirm in the contract that the Webflow or Framer site is transferred to the company's own workspace, and that Figma files, fonts and image licences are handed over at the end of the project.

Should copy come from the same agency as the design?

One team for copy and design removes a handoff in the middle of the project, and half of agencies include copywriting. The benchmark's author still recommends a freelance direct-response copywriter when an agency has no dedicated writer. Ask who writes and see samples before deciding.

Can a new website improve SaaS conversion rates?

Yes, when the redesign fixes the actual cause: unclear positioning, weak proof, a confusing path to demo or trial, or slow pages. After its website redesign, Scale.jobs' visitor-to-lead conversion rose from 1.2% to 2.8% in the first month. A visual refresh alone rarely moves conversion, so ask agencies for before-and-after numbers from SaaS clients.

When should a SaaS company redesign its website?

The most common triggers in 100 sales calls were an outdated site or rebrand (21 calls) and a new product or launch (19). A raise, a move upmarket, or a site that marketing cannot update without engineering are the other usual signals. A site that no longer matches how the sales team describes the product is overdue.

Does a Series A company need an agency or an in-house designer?

For the relaunch, an agency is faster and brings a full team; 41% of benchmarked agencies staff a project with three to five people. After launch, many Series A companies keep the agency on a retainer or hire one designer to run the system the agency built.

Do time zones matter when hiring a design agency?

They came up in 20 of 100 sales calls. 60% of benchmarked agencies are based in Europe and 20% in Asia, so US teams often work with a partial-day overlap. That works when feedback is consolidated and asynchronous, and breaks down when a project needs daily live reviews.

Is AI replacing web designers?

AI tools now handle layouts, flows and much of the build. What they do not supply is taste and judgement about what to say, to whom and in what order. Many AI-built sites now look alike, which raises the value of a distinctive one.

Do AI SaaS companies need an AI-specialist agency?

Not necessarily, but they need an agency that can make an abstract, fast-changing product concrete and trustworthy. The AI UX agencies guide covers that specialism.
