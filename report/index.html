1. Methodology

The price of design work is usually hidden behind a 30-minute sales call per agency. To make those numbers comparable, a SaaS client exploring agencies went through the sales process with 57 UI/UX design agencies, close to 30 hours of calls, and asked each one the questions a buyer would ask before hiring.

Every agency received the same documented scope:

BriefScope
Brand refreshA refresh documented in a brand book: new colours, typography and brand elements for marketing material. No logo design.
WebsiteA 5-page website: design, copywriting and development on Webflow or Framer.
MVPA 5-feature MVP: user research, design, user testing and QA.

How to read the numbers:

  • Not every agency answered every question. Shares are of the agencies that answered, and the number of answers is shown where it is known.
  • Where agencies could give more than one answer (platforms, for example), shares add up to more than 100%.
  • "Personal thought" notes are the author's interpretation of the data, kept separate from the findings.

2. Pricing

2.1 Brand refresh

Median $15,300. Quotes ran from $1,500 to $125,000.

PriceShare of agencies
Under $10,00042%
$10,000 to $25,00033%
Over $25,00025%

Personal thought: Without a logo, a brand refresh is mostly documentation. Ask what the quote adds beyond colours and type (messaging, templates, a full brand book) before comparing totals.

2.2 Website (5 pages)

Median $25,000. Quotes ran from $1,500 to $120,000.

PriceShare of agencies
Under $10,00025%
$15,000 to $50,00058%
$50,000 to $100,000+17%

The spread comes from what each agency read into the same brief. A simple site with no animation draws a lower quote; motion, 3D and custom builds push it up.

Personal thought: A single landing page with no motion can be designed and built for under $10,000. Anything larger scales with pages, motion and custom work, so price each of those separately.

2.3 Product design (MVP)

60% of agencies would not quote up front. They needed the exact scope and expectations first. Expect a product design estimate of 1.5 to 2.5 times the website estimate.

ModelLowestMedianHighest
Fixed price$2,500about $43,000over $150,000
Monthly subscriptionabout $4,000about $7,000$20,000

Personal thought: If the scope is still open and you want to explore directions, a retainer removes the friction of going back and forth, because the agency is paid for its time. If you know exactly what you want, a fixed price gives you a predictable budget.

2.4 Hourly rates

Median $55 to $90 an hour. The lowest rate quoted was $25 an hour and the highest $195, from a senior US-based team. Some agencies quote a day rate, around $900.

Personal thought: Asian agencies have the lowest rates, then European agencies, then US agencies. Rate and quality did not move together in these calls.

4. Who the agencies are

4.1 Location

60% are based in Europe, 20% in the Americas and 20% in Asia. The agencies came from North America (US and Canada), Eastern Europe (Ukraine and Poland), Western Europe (UK and Portugal) and Asia (India, Bangladesh and Indonesia).

Personal thought: US agencies are the most expensive (about $150 an hour on average), European agencies come second (about $60) and Asian agencies are the most affordable (about $35).

4.2 Team size

Most agencies are mid-size.

TeamShare of agencies
Boutique, under 15 people29%
Mid-size, 16 to 50 people48%
Large, over 50 people23%

Personal thought: The experience and seniority of the people on your project matter more than the total headcount.

4.3 Designer-to-client ratio

Most agencies run 1 to 2 designers per client (16 answers).

Designers per clientShare of agencies
One designer per client6.25%
1 to 2 designers per client62.5%
More than 2 designers per client31.25%

Personal thought: This shows how stretched a designer's time is, and how much of it your project will get.

4.4 In-house vs contractors

70% work with a mainly in-house team. 20% use a core team plus trusted long-term contractors, and 10% partner with other agencies for services such as development.

Personal thought: Fully in-house teams are usually more expensive. Long-term contractors hired per project also work well, as long as the same people stay on your project.

4.5 Remote, hybrid or office

60% are fully remote or remote-first. 30% are hybrid and 10% work mainly from an office, usually with some remote staff.

Personal thought: This should not decide your choice; most agencies have remote team members either way.

4.6 Industries and company stage

The most common specialisations were AI and B2B SaaS, followed by fintech and healthcare, with edtech and martech also frequent. On company stage, most agencies said they work with companies of every size.

Personal thought: Check the client names in an agency's portfolio for companies at a similar stage to yours.

5. What a quote includes

5.1 Platforms

65% build on Webflow. 25% build on WordPress, 10% on Framer, and 20% design only, with no development. Agencies could name more than one platform.

Personal thought: Webflow offers the most flexibility and scale for marketing sites. If you have a platform preference, settle it with the agency before the proposal.

5.2 Development

55% develop in-house, as a one-stop shop. 45% rely on trusted external partners and focus on design.

Personal thought: When development sits with a partner, problems in the build can fall between two companies. One team for design and build is simpler to manage.

5.3 Copywriting

A 50/50 split. Half include copywriting, which keeps the message and the design aligned. Half expect the client to supply copy, which gives more control over voice but adds to your workload.

Personal thought: Copy can make or break a website's conversion rate. Ask who writes it and what direct-response experience they have; if the agency does not write copy, bring in a specialist.

5.4 UX research and testing

Over 95% call UX research core to their process. The most common methods are competitor analysis, stakeholder interviews and user interviews. About 70% also offer moderated usability testing with real users on prototypes.

Personal thought: "Research" can mean almost anything. Ask open-ended questions about how the agency runs it to judge how deep it goes.

5.5 CRO

About 80% offer conversion rate optimisation. Approaches range from analysing data in Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity to A/B testing and information architecture. About 20% do not offer CRO as a service. Every agency described its websites as conversion-focused, and a majority treated CRO as part of SEO.

Personal thought: If conversion is the main goal, choose an agency that can show how it understands your customers and measures conversion, not one that only promises it.

6.2 Client commitment

About 5 hours a week from your main point of contact, especially at the start, was the most specific estimate given. Every agency expected timely feedback, joining kickoff workshops and at least one weekly sync call.

6.3 Communication

100% use Slack and Figma. Over 90% combine daily updates on Slack with one or two scheduled meetings a week.

6.4 Developer handoff

Nearly 100% hand off an organised Figma file with a full design system. Over 80% involve the client's developers early to validate technical choices, and nearly all offer design QA after handoff so the build matches the design.

7. Hiring checklist

Use the same questions with every agency, so the quotes can be compared line by line.

  1. Scope: what exactly is included for each page, and what is extra?
  2. Development: is the build done in-house or by a partner?
  3. Copy: who writes it, and with what experience?
  4. Research: what does research mean here, and who talks to users?
  5. Team: who sits on the project pod, and how senior are they?
  6. Timeline: what is each phase for, and when can work start?
  7. Payment: time and materials, fixed price or retainer, and what are the terms?
  8. After launch: retainer, hourly support or a bank of hours?
  9. Your side: can you give about 5 hours a week, a weekly sync and quick feedback on Slack?

8. Conclusion

The number an agency quotes has little to do with the work requested. With the same scope, a brand refresh came back anywhere from $1,500 to $125,000 (median $15,300) and a 5-page website from $1,500 to $120,000 (median $25,000). For product design, 60% of agencies would not put a number on it before another conversation, and the rest ranged from $2,500 to over $150,000 on a fixed price, or $4,000 to $20,000 a month on subscription. Hourly rates ran from $25 to $195, with a median of $55 to $90.

Timelines vary for a similar reason. A brand refresh lands at 4 to 8 weeks for 54% of agencies. Websites stretch further: 43.5% quoted 5 to 9 weeks and 34.8% quoted 10 weeks or more. MVP design takes 10 weeks or more at 52.6% of agencies. Longer timelines usually come from rigid internal processes rather than better work. 90% of agencies can run website and product design in parallel, and 90% can start within four weeks of signing.

Quotes are hard to compare because they include different things. 20% of agencies design only, and 45% hand development to an external partner. Copywriting is split 50/50. Over 95% call research core, and about 80% offer CRO, so ask how each is actually done. What matters on the team side is the pod on your project, which is 3 to 5 people at 41% of agencies, and how thinly its designers are spread.

So compare the specifics, not the pitch deck. Get the scope line by line, match the payment model to how well you know your scope, agree support after launch before you sign, and plan for your own time on the project.

9. FAQs

How much does a website cost from a UI/UX design agency in 2026?

For a 5-page website with copywriting and a Webflow or Framer build, the median quote was $25,000. Quotes ran from $1,500 to $120,000, and 58% of agencies quoted between $15,000 and $50,000.

How much does a brand refresh cost?

Without a logo, the median quote was $15,300, with a range of $1,500 to $125,000. 42% of agencies quoted under $10,000.

How much does MVP design cost?

The median fixed quote was about $43,000, with a range of $2,500 to over $150,000. Subscriptions ran from about $4,000 to $20,000 a month, with a median of about $7,000. 60% of agencies would not quote without another call.

How long does a website take?

43.5% of agencies quoted 5 to 9 weeks, 21.7% quoted 1 to 4 weeks and 34.8% quoted 10 weeks or more. 45% can start within two weeks of signing.

What do design agencies charge per hour?

From $25 to $195 an hour, with a median of $55 to $90. US agencies average about $150 an hour, European agencies about $60 and Asian agencies about $35.

Which platform do most design agencies build on?

65% build on Webflow, 25% on WordPress and 10% on Framer. 20% design only and do not build.

Fixed price, time and materials or retainer?

45% of agencies offer time and materials, 35% fixed price and 20% a retainer or subscription. A fixed price suits a clear scope; a retainer suits work where the scope is still being explored.

How are design agencies usually paid?

50% take an upfront deposit followed by milestone payments. 30% bill monthly for hours worked, 10% bill upfront per phase and 10% split the total into equal monthly payments.
